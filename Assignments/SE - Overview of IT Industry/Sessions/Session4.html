1. Six Phases of SDLC

  1. Planning: Define the project goals, budget, and timeline.
  2. Requirement Analysis: Collect and analyze user requirements.
  3. Design: Plan the software architecture and user interface.
  4. Development: Write the code to build the software.
  5. Testing: Find bugs and check that the software works correctly.
  6. Maintenance: Fix issues and update the software after release.

2. Three Differences Between Agile and Waterfall

Agile Waterfall
Development happens in short iterations. Development follows sequential phases.
Requirements can change during development. Requirements are usually defined early.
Testing and feedback happen regularly. Testing usually happens after development.

3. Three Main Scrum Roles

  1. Product Owner: Prioritizes the work and manages the Product Backlog.
  2. Scrum Master: Helps the team follow Scrum and removes obstacles.
  3. Developers: Build and deliver a usable product increment.

4. What Is a Sprint in Scrum?

A Sprint is a fixed period in which the Scrum Team works to create a usable product increment.

Typical duration: 1 to 4 weeks, with a maximum of one month.

5. Meaning of Jira Board Columns

  • To Do: Tasks that are waiting to be started.
  • In Progress: Tasks that are currently being worked on.
  • Done: Tasks that have been completed.
  • Backlog: Tasks and ideas planned for future work.
